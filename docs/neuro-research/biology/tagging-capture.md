# tagging-capture: Synaptic Tagging and Capture (Frey & Morris): local tags, plasticity-related protein capture, behavioral tagging, and the credit-assignment logic of the consolidation window

## Summary
According to PubMed-verified sources: LTP has two phases. Early-LTP is induced locally at a synapse, needs no protein synthesis, and decays in ~1-3 hours. Late-LTP requires transcription and translation in the soma/nucleus. This creates a routing problem: new proteins are made cell-wide, yet persistence is synapse-specific. Frey & Morris (1997) solved it with a two-token protocol. Any supra-threshold induction — even a weak tetanus that alone yields only decaying early-LTP — sets a local, protein-synthesis-independent SYNAPTIC TAG at exactly the stimulated synapses. A strong event anywhere else on the same neuron triggers synthesis of plasticity-related proteins (PRPs) that diffuse cell-wide with no address. Consolidation is the intersection: only synapses holding a live tag CAPTURE the PRPs and convert transient change into lasting change. The tag is the address; the PRP wave is an unaddressed broadcast; persistence = AND(tag_set, PRPs_available) evaluated over overlapping decay windows. The tag decays in under 3 hours — rescue works at 5 min and 1 h separation but fails by 2-4 h (Frey & Morris 1998, Neuropharmacology) — and critically the logic is ORDER-INDEPENDENT: weak-before-strong works as well as strong-before-weak, so a strong event retroactively consolidates recently tagged weak events. Mechanistically the tag is a state, not one molecule: CaMKII activity is required for tag setting while the CaMKK cascade governs PRP synthesis/availability — pharmacologically dissociable processes (Redondo et al. 2010); the revised hypothesis (Redondo & Morris 2011) separates tag-setting from LTP expression itself. PRP synthesis is gated by neuromodulators: dopamine D1/D5 (aminergic) activation is required, so behavioral salience/novelty decides whether the broadcast happens at all (Frey & Morris 1998, TINS; Wang et al. 2010). One identified PRP, the constitutively active kinase PKMzeta, maintains potentiation and is captured only by LTP tags, not LTD tags — tags are PROCESS-SPECIFIC while the PRP pool is shared, producing "cross-tagging" where late-LTP in one pathway converts early-LTD elsewhere into late-LTD and vice versa (Sajikumar & Frey 2004; Sajikumar et al. 2005). The behavioral translation is exact: weak inhibitory-avoidance or spatial training that alone yields only short-term memory becomes long-term memory if the animal explores a NOVEL (not familiar) environment within roughly an hour before or after training; the rescue requires hippocampal D1/D5 receptors and new protein synthesis, and generalizes across hippocampal and non-hippocampal tasks — "behavioral tagging" (Moncada & Viola 2007; Ballarini et al. 2009; Wang et al. 2010). Two refinements sharpen the computational picture. First, capture is spatially biased: local dendritic translation makes the dendritic branch the capture compartment, so tagged synapses on the branch where PRPs are made win the competition for a limited PRP supply — memories co-allocated in time cluster on shared branches (Govindarajan, Kelleher & Tonegawa 2006). Second, there is INVERSE tagging: activity-induced Arc/Arg3.1 protein preferentially accumulates at INACTIVE synapses by binding calmodulin-free CaMKIIbeta, driving AMPA-receptor removal there — so during a cell-wide plasticity event, untagged/inactive synapses are actively weakened rather than merely ignored, preventing weak synapses from free-riding on the broadcast and enhancing signal-to-noise (Okuno et al. 2012). The system is thus a complete credit-assignment design: a cheap, decaying, synapse-local eligibility trace written optimistically at every event; an expensive, salience-gated, cell-wide commit; consolidation as the time-windowed conjunction of the two; process-typed tags over a shared resource pool; branch-level locality creating association-by-capture-competition; and a complementary negative tag that demotes non-participants during each commit.

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Synaptic tag setting (local eligibility trace)**: Signal: any supra-threshold induction, even weak. Tag: a protein-synthesis-independent local state change at exactly the activated synapse (CaMKII-dependent to set). Threshold: low — set optimistically by events too weak to persist on their own. Timescale: decays in <3 h (rescue robust at 5 min-1 h, gone by 2-4 h). The tag records WHERE credit belongs without committing resources.
- **PRP synthesis broadcast (cell-wide commit)**: Signal: strong tetanization or salient/novel experience. Mechanism: nuclear transcription + translation of plasticity-related proteins (PRPs; PKMzeta is one identified LTP-specific PRP), distributed cell-wide with NO addressing. Threshold: high, and additionally gated by dopamine D1/D5 / aminergic input — no salience, no broadcast. Timescale: PRP availability lasts hours; CaMKK cascade governs supply.
- **Tag-AND-capture intersection (order-independent consolidation window)**: Persistence = AND(tag alive, PRPs present) within overlapping decay windows. Weak-before-strong works as well as strong-before-weak, so a later salient event RETROACTIVELY consolidates recently tagged weak events. This is eligibility-trace credit assignment: broadcast plus local filter replaces per-synapse routing.
- **Process-specific tags over a shared PRP pool (cross-tagging)**: LTP tags and LTD tags are distinct types, but both draw on one shared PRP pool; PKMzeta is captured only by LTP tags. Consequence: late-LTP in one input converts early-LTD in another into late-LTD and vice versa — heterosynaptic association between opposite-sign changes, i.e., typed capture from an untyped broadcast.
- **Behavioral tagging (novelty rescues weak memories)**: Weak training sets a learning tag yielding only short-term memory; unrelated NOVEL (not familiar) experience within ~±1 h supplies PRPs (dopamine- and protein-synthesis-dependent), promoting STM to LTM. Familiarity fails — the gate detects prediction error/novelty, not activity per se. Generalizes across task types and brain regions.
- **Clustered capture via local dendritic translation**: PRPs can be translated locally in dendrites; the dendritic branch is the capture compartment. Tagged synapses compete for a LIMITED PRP supply, and proximity to the synthesis site biases the winner — so memories formed close in time cluster onto shared branches, converting a resource constraint into an association mechanism.
- **Inverse synaptic tagging (Arc/CaMKIIbeta demotion of non-participants)**: During strong cell-wide activation, induced Arc protein binds calmodulin-FREE CaMKIIbeta — a state found only at inactive synapses — accumulating there and driving AMPA-receptor endocytosis. Logic: each commit event also actively weakens synapses that did NOT participate, normalizing contrast and preventing free-riding on the broadcast.
- **Neuromodulatory salience gate on consolidation**: Dopamine D1/D5 (aminergic) receptor activation is a hard prerequisite for the PRP broadcast: blockade prevents late-LTP and memory persistence even with strong induction; novelty exploration restores it. A global scalar 'this period matters' signal decides whether ANY tagged event gets consolidated — salience is the commit authorization, separate from content.

## ETTZ relevance notes from researcher
STC speaks directly to the owner's question 'synaptic tagging = flow refs or claims' trials?' — the honest answer is neither. Flow refs are permanent identity threads (axonal tracing); claims' trials are slow evidence accumulation (Hebbian counters). The synaptic tag is a third thing ETTZ currently lacks: a cheap, DECAYING, item-local eligibility marker written optimistically at every event, whose only job is to let a later salient event retroactively promote it. This lands squarely on the suspected sharpest gap (no automatic episodic-to-claims/procedures promotion). An STC-shaped design: every envelope ingested gets an implicit eligibility window (tag, ~hours not 30 days — distinct from entropy's dwell clock); a high-salience event (anomaly envelope, owner CHOICE envelope, failed-claim trial, novelty per the ignorance graph) plays the strong tetanus and authorizes a consolidation pass — a salience-TRIGGERED mini-fold, complementing the circadian memory-nightly (which maps to sleep replay, not to PRP capture; STC argues nightly cadence is too coarse for the 1-2 h window). Promotion = AND(tag alive, salience event fired) — broadcast + local filter, so no per-item routing is needed; and because envelopes are append-only with timestamps, the order-independence (weak-BEFORE-strong) that biology buys with a physical tag, ETTZ gets free by scanning backward over the log within the window. Behavioral tagging maps cleanly: a routine envelope that would fade earns durability because a surprising envelope landed nearby in time — a concrete rule for which episodic patterns memory-nightly should promote into claims/procedures. The dopamine D1/D5 gate is the design lesson that salience must be a separate commit-authorization signal, not inferred from content volume — ETTZ's why-ledger and CHOICE envelopes already carry candidate salience signals. Process-specific tags over a shared PRP pool (cross-tagging) licenses one consolidation pass serving typed outcomes (strengthen claims, close windows, register procedures) from one shared trigger. Clustered capture (competition for limited PRPs within a branch) suggests consolidation budget per fold with locality bias via flow refs — items sharing a flow ref should co-promote, giving association-by-capture. Inverse tagging answers the pruning-law question: biology's demotion of non-participants (Arc removes receptors, does not destroy the synapse) is compatible with 'compress and archive, never erase' — each promotion pass could mark untouched siblings in the same window for earlier entropy/quarantine review, a weakening not a deletion. Finally, Redondo & Morris's revision (induction creates only the POTENTIAL for change, not the commitment) is exactly the two-phase-commit shape ETTZ's trigger fabric already uses (act, then confirming envelope closes the loop) — extended here to memory durability itself.
