# growth-cones: Growth cones, filopodia and lamellipodia in axon guidance and dendritic spine formation: exploratory actin dynamics, guidance-cue gradients (netrins, semaphorins, ephrins), chemoattraction/repulsion, target selection, and dendritic filopodia as spine precursors

## Summary
The growth cone is a self-steering actuator at the tip of a growing axon, built from two actin machines: a broad lamellipodium (branched actin mesh) and finger-like filopodia (bundles of parallel actin filaments, up to ~10 um long). The engine is an actin treadmill: G-actin polymerizes at the leading-edge membrane while myosin II pulls the whole filament array rearward (retrograde flow, um/min). By default this treadmill spins in place. Forward motion requires a molecular clutch: when adhesion receptors bind substrate ligands, they mechanically couple the flowing actin to the outside world, converting flow into protrusive traction (Mitchison & Kirschner 1988; Lowery & Van Vactor 2009). Steering is therefore not a steering wheel but spatially asymmetric clutch engagement — traction is granted only on the side where signals validate it.

Exploration is done by filopodia acting as disposable antennae. They extend and retract stochastically on a minutes timescale, each carrying guidance receptors; a filopodium that contacts a positive cue is selectively stabilized and the growth cone's mass follows it. Bentley & Toroian-Raymond (1986) showed the dissociation cleanly: cytochalasin-treated pioneer growth cones without filopodia still elongate but become disoriented — exploration and locomotion are separable subsystems; filopodia carry the navigation, not the propulsion.

Sensing is a spatial differencing operation. The growth cone (10-50 um wide) compares receptor occupancy across its own span and can steer up gradients with concentration differences of order 0.1% across its width — but only inside a narrow absolute concentration band, implying gain control/adaptation that re-zeros the detector as ambient levels change (Rosoff et al. 2004). Long routes are broken into legs between intermediate targets, each leg a fresh gradient problem.

The cue lexicon is small and reused (Kolodkin & Tessier-Lavigne 2011). Netrin-1 (homolog of C. elegans UNC-6) is a floor-plate-secreted diffusible attractant for commissural axons (Serafini et al. 1994) via the DCC receptor; the same ligand repels axons expressing UNC5 receptors. Semaphorins are dedicated repellents: collapsin-1/Sema3A at ~10 pM triggers actin depolymerization and collapse of specific growth cones while sparing others (Luo et al. 1993) — a receptor-gated veto, and asymmetric partial collapse is turning. Crucially, valence is not a property of the ligand: raising or lowering cAMP/cGMP in the growth cone converts repulsion to attraction for the identical cue (Song et al. 1998). The sign is computed at the receiver from (ligand, receptor complement, second-messenger state).

Target selection in the retinotectal map uses matched analog gradients: ephrin-A ligands (e.g., RAGS/ephrin-A2) form a low-to-high anterior-posterior gradient on the tectum and repel retinal axons carrying graded EphA receptor levels (Drescher et al. 1995); each axon stops where repulsion crosses its own receptor-scaled threshold — an address computed from relative, not absolute, values, so the map rescales automatically.

Dendrites run the mirror-image search. Young dendrites are covered with transient filopodia (mean lifetime ~9.5 min) that sample axons within a 5-10 um radius; contacts that recruit a functional presynaptic bouton are stabilized and the filopodium matures into a spine (Ziv & Smith 1996). The commit test is fast and local: within seconds of touching an axon, local dendritic calcium transients begin, and high transient frequency predicts stabilization while low frequency predicts retraction; contacts with the wrong partner class (certain GABAergic axons) are never stabilized (Lohmann & Bonhoeffer 2008) — a handshake protocol with partner-type filtering executed before any mature synapse exists. At arbor scale this becomes the synaptotropic model: in vivo, nearly all new synapses form on newly extended filopodia, retained synapses stabilize their filopodium into a branch, and iteration steers arbor growth toward territory where connections succeed (Niell et al. 2004). The architecture is uniform across scales: cheap, fast, majority-fail probes; validation by local signaling; commitment of expensive durable structure only where probes are confirmed.

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Actin treadmill with molecular clutch**: Signal: substrate ligand binding to adhesion receptors. Tag: clutch engagement mechanically coupling retrograde-flowing F-actin (myosin-II driven, ~um/min) to the substrate. Threshold: enough engaged clutches to convert flow into traction. Timescale: seconds-minutes. Computation: a constantly running motor whose output is gated locally — direction emerges from WHERE traction is validated, not from a central steering command.
- **Filopodial stochastic sampling with selective stabilization**: Signal: guidance-cue receptor binding on individual filopodia. Tag: the stabilized filopodium (cytoskeletal reinforcement) marks the winning direction. Threshold: one filopodium's cue contact outcompeting siblings. Timescale: filopodia live minutes; most fail and retract (material recycled, not destroyed). Computation: parallel speculative probes with reinforce-the-winner; exploration is separable from locomotion — without filopodia the axon still moves but cannot navigate.
- **Spatial gradient comparison with adaptive gain control**: Signal: differential receptor occupancy across the growth cone's 10-50 um span. Threshold: ~0.1% concentration difference detectable, but only within a narrow absolute concentration band. Timescale: turning decisions in minutes; adaptation re-zeros the setpoint as ambient concentration shifts. Computation: a spatial differentiator with automatic gain control; long routes decomposed into legs between intermediate targets, each leg a fresh local gradient problem.
- **Receiver-computed cue valence (sign switching)**: Signal: one ligand (e.g., netrin-1). Tag: receptor complement (DCC = attract, UNC5-containing = repel) plus internal second-messenger state (cAMP/cGMP levels flip repulsion to attraction for the identical cue). Timescale: state changes act within minutes. Computation: valence is a policy lookup keyed on (ligand, receptor set, internal state) — the environment broadcasts neutral data; each receiver decides its own sign.
- **Repulsive collapse as receptor-gated veto**: Signal: semaphorin/collapsin at ~10 pM triggers actin depolymerization. Tag: receptor expression determines which growth cones are sensitive (sensory yes, retinal no). Threshold: picomolar — vetoes are far more sensitive than go-signals. Timescale: collapse in minutes; asymmetric partial collapse = turning. Computation: pruning of candidate directions rather than commanding one; navigation largely by subtraction of forbidden options.
- **Topographic address matching via paired analog gradients**: Signal: tectal ephrin-A repellent gradient read by axons carrying graded EphA receptor levels. Threshold: each axon terminates where repulsion crosses its own receptor-scaled setpoint. Timescale: developmental (days), refined by activity. Computation: target address encoded as a RELATIVE value match between two gradients — no absolute coordinates, so the map auto-rescales to target size.
- **Dendritic filopodia handshake: contact, calcium test, commit**: Signal: physical filopodium-axon contact (sampling radius 5-10 um) evokes local dendritic calcium transients within seconds. Tag: transient FREQUENCY at the contact site. Threshold: high frequency -> stabilize into spine; low frequency -> retract; wrong partner class (specific GABAergic axons) never stabilizes. Timescale: filopodium lifetime ~9.5 min; spine maturation hours-days. Computation: a trial-based handshake that scores connection quality and filters partner identity BEFORE committing durable structure.
- **Synaptotropic growth: structure follows validated connections**: Signal: a nascent synapse forming on a newly extended filopodium. Tag: the retained synapse stabilizes its host filopodium, which matures into a branch. Threshold: synapse maintenance vs elimination decides branch fate. Timescale: iterated over hours-days of arborization. Computation: a growth loop gated by confirmed connections — the arbor invests only where probes returned successful handshakes, steering growth toward connection-rich territory.

## ETTZ relevance notes from researcher
The filopodium is the direct answer to the owner's 'what actively explores' question, and its logic is precise enough to copy: cheap, ephemeral, majority-fail probes (minutes-scale TTL), spawned stochastically, each carrying the receptors needed to test one candidate connection, with stabilization — not creation — as the expensive, selective step. ETTZ has no such organ: watch-contracts proposes joins from published contracts (a slow, deliberate process), but nothing speculatively probes for new cross-organ links or claim-claim connections and lets most probes die. Notably, biology's retraction recycles actin monomers rather than destroying identity, which is compatible in spirit with 'compress and archive, never erase' — a failed probe could collapse to a single choice-envelope ('probed X, no confirmation, retracted') rather than needing quarantine. Bentley's dissociation (elongation without steering) warns that pipelines keep running without exploration — ETTZ would not halt without a filopodia analog; it would just navigate blindly. The molecular clutch maps onto the trigger fabric's confirming envelopes: progress only where acknowledgment validates traction. Receiver-computed valence (DCC/UNC5, cAMP switch) endorses the why-ledger design: the signal is neutral data; the judgment (sign) belongs to the receiver and its state, exactly where CHOICE envelopes put it. Ephrin gradients argue that any ranking built on usage BALANCES should use relative, self-rescaling comparisons, not absolute thresholds. Rosoff's narrow-band sensitivity plus adaptation parallels BEATS drift analysis: exquisite differential sensitivity requires continuous re-zeroing against baseline. The dendritic handshake (contact, seconds-scale calcium trial, frequency threshold, commit or retract) is structurally identical to claims' trials (+confirm/-fail) applied at connection level, and Lohmann's partner-class filtering suggests trials should gate flow-ref promotion. Most importantly for the suspected sharpest gap: synaptotropic growth IS the missing episodic-to-durable promotion loop — repeated successful contacts (episodic events) automatically become durable structure (spine, branch), meaning memory-nightly could promote recurring confirmed contact patterns into claims/procedures by exactly this reinforce-where-validated rule.
