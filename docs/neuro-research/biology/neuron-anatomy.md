# neuron-anatomy: Soma vs dendrites vs axon division of labour as computation: dendritic integration and branch plasticity, the soma as trophic/final-integration hub, the axon initial segment as decision threshold, axonal transport as logistics, and compartmentalized death (soma death vs branch pruning)

## Summary
Based on articles retrieved from PubMed, the neuron is not a point summator; it is a three-stage pipeline with distinct compute, decide, and ship/supply subsystems. DENDRITES are the input layer and do real computation. Passively, they are leaky RC cables: each synaptic current is attenuated and low-pass filtered with distance, summation is sublinear (driving-force saturation), and the membrane time constant (~10-30 ms) sets the base integration window (London & Hausser 2005; Stuart & Spruston 2015). Actively, thin terminal branches hold NMDA receptors plus voltage-gated Na/Ca channels that turn each branch into a nonlinear subunit: roughly 10-20 synapses co-activated within ~20-40 um of each other inside a few milliseconds cross a regenerative threshold and fire a local NMDA spike, a 25-100 ms plateau confined mostly to that branch (Major, Larkum & Schiller 2013). Computationally the cell behaves as a two-layer network: dozens of sigmoidal branch subunits feed a final thresholding unit at the soma/axon (Poirazi et al. 2003). A second nonlinearity sits in the apical tuft of pyramidal cells: a Cav-channel Ca2+ spike zone whose threshold drops sharply when a backpropagated somatic action potential arrives within ~30 ms of distal input (BAC firing), converting coincidence of top-down and bottom-up streams into a burst instead of a single spike — an associative AND gate built into the cell (Larkum 2013). Plasticity is addressed at branch granularity: NMDAR-dependent downregulation of Kv4.2 A-type K+ channels potentiates a whole branch's spike-to-soma coupling ("branch strength potentiation", Losonczy et al. 2008), and in vivo different motor tasks write to different tuft branches, with somatostatin interneurons enforcing the branch-level separation that prevents interference between memories (Cichon & Gan 2015). The SOMA holds the nucleus and most ribosomes: it is the transcription/translation factory, the trophic hub, and the last analog summing node before the decision element. The decision itself is exported ~20-60 um down the axon to the AXON INITIAL SEGMENT (AIS): an ankyrin-G scaffold anchors voltage-gated Na+ channels at ~50x somatodendritic density (~2500 pS/um^2), making the AIS the lowest-threshold point and the single, privileged analog-to-digital converter of the cell (Kole et al. 2008; Kole & Stuart 2012). The threshold is not fixed hardware: with days of chronic depolarization the entire AIS relocates up to ~17 um distally, raising current threshold — a homeostatic tuning knob operating on an hours-to-days timescale, far slower than synaptic plasticity (Grubb & Burrone 2010). The AIS is simultaneously the identity gate: it forms a diffusion barrier and cytoplasmic filter that keeps axonal and somatodendritic proteins segregated; losing it makes an axon revert to dendritic character (Rasband 2010). The AXON is output plus logistics. Because nearly all protein synthesis is somatic, survival of an axon meters out over microtubule-based transport: kinesins run anterograde fast transport (~1 um/s; membrane organelles, synaptic precursors), dynein runs retrograde transport returning aged proteins for degradation and carrying signaling endosomes with trophic information back to the nucleus; cytoskeletal cargo moves by slow transport (mm/day) (Maday et al. 2014). Death is compartmentalized and asymmetric. Soma death (apoptosis) is global: the arbor, axon, and all synapses go with it. Axon/branch death does not require soma death: development prunes exuberant axons and branches while the parent neuron survives (Luo & O'Leary 2005), and injured or undersupplied axon segments execute a local, actively triggered program — Wallerian degeneration — governed by a dead-man's switch: the labile survival factor NMNAT2 (half-life of hours) must be continuously delivered from the soma; when delivery stops, NMN/NAD+ balance shifts, the NADase SARM1 activates, and the distal segment self-destructs within days, entirely locally (Coleman & Hoke 2020). The architecture is thus: distributed nonlinear pre-processing with branch-addressable plasticity; one centralized factory and identity; one movable threshold; supply lines whose silence is itself the kill signal; and deletion that is scoped to compartments, never to identity — unless the factory itself dies.

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Passive cable integration (dendritic RC filtering)**: Signal: synaptic current. Transform: distance-dependent attenuation + low-pass filtering along a leaky cable; sublinear summation from driving-force saturation. Threshold: none — analog. Timescale: membrane time constant ~10-30 ms sets the coincidence window; location on the tree encodes delay and weight. Computation: weighted, lossy, order- and place-sensitive analog summation before any nonlinearity.
- **NMDA spikes in thin branches (clustered-input detector)**: Signal: glutamate binding + local depolarization unblocking NMDAR Mg2+ gates (voltage-AND-ligand gate). Threshold: ~10-20 co-active synapses within ~20-40 um and a few ms. Output: regenerative plateau 25-100 ms confined to the branch. Computation: each terminal branch is a sigmoidal subunit firing only on spatiotemporally CLUSTERED input — a pattern detector, not a counter; the whole cell is a two-layer network (branch subunits -> somatic threshold).
- **Apical Ca2+ spike / BAC firing (coincidence AND gate)**: Signal: distal tuft input (top-down/context) and a backpropagating somatic action potential (bottom-up/output echo). Threshold: Cav-channel Ca2+ spike threshold in the apical zone, sharply lowered when both signals coincide within ~30 ms. Output: burst instead of single spike — a distinguishable 'both streams agree' code. Computation: cellular-level association of feedback and feedforward information; inhibitory interneurons targeting the tuft act as a veto line.
- **Branch-specific plasticity and memory allocation**: Tag: the branch, not only the synapse. Mechanism: NMDAR-dependent downregulation of Kv4.2 A-type K+ channels raises a branch's spike-to-soma coupling gain (branch strength potentiation); in vivo, different tasks generate Ca2+ spikes on different branches, and somatostatin interneuron gating keeps task-writes on separate branches. Computation: plasticity addressed at an intermediate granularity — a routing/gain table per branch — providing interference avoidance by allocating new learning to unused subunits.
- **Axon initial segment as the single decision threshold**: Substrate: ankyrin-G scaffold clustering Na+ channels at ~50x somatic density (~2500 pS/um^2), 20-60 um from the soma. Threshold: lowest in the cell (~-55 mV); all dendritic analog computation is serialized through this one comparator into an all-or-none digital event. Plasticity: the whole structure physically relocates (up to ~17 um) or resizes over hours-days of altered activity, homeostatically retuning threshold — slow adaptation of the decision element itself, decoupled from fast synaptic learning.
- **AIS as identity/polarity gate**: Mechanism: the AIS forms a membrane diffusion barrier and an intracellular traffic filter that admits axonal cargo and excludes somatodendritic cargo. Computation: a single choke point enforces compartment identity (what counts as 'output wiring' vs 'input tree'); losing the gate causes the axon to lose its identity and revert to dendrite-like state. Timescale: continuously maintained for the life of the cell.
- **Axonal transport as metered logistics with return channel**: Anterograde: kinesin motors, fast component ~1 um/s (~50-400 mm/day) for organelles/synaptic material; slow component mm/day for cytoskeleton. Retrograde: dynein returns aged proteins/autophagosomes for somatic degradation and carries signaling endosomes (trophic receipts) back to the nucleus. Computation: a bidirectional, cargo-typed queue where the outbound lane is supply, the inbound lane is both quality control and acknowledgment/telemetry; nearly all manufacturing stays centralized at the soma.
- **Compartmentalized death: apoptosis vs Wallerian program vs pruning**: Soma death (apoptosis) = global deletion of the entire cell and arbor. Branch/axon pruning (development) = scoped deletion; parent neuron survives. Wallerian degeneration = a LOCAL executable: survival factor NMNAT2 has an hours-scale half-life and must be continuously shipped from the soma; cessation of delivery shifts NMN/NAD+, activating the NADase SARM1, which destroys the distal segment within days without somatic involvement. Logic: axon survival is a lease renewed by delivery — silence on the supply line IS the death signal; the threshold is a metabolite ratio, the timescale is hours (arming) to days (execution).

## Citations
- London M, Hausser M (2005). Dendritic computation. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*. PMID:16033324. [verified] — supports: Dendrites implement linear and nonlinear elementary computations (passive cable filtering, sublinear summation, local nonlinear mechanisms) rather than merely relaying input to the soma; foundational review of the dendrite-as-computer view.
- Stuart GJ, Spruston N (2015). Dendritic integration: 60 years of progress. *Nature Neuroscience*. PMID:26605882. [verified] — supports: Neurons perform multiple passive and active forms of synaptic integration, confirmed in vitro and in vivo including awake animals; dendrites are critical to single-neuron information processing.
- Major G, Larkum ME, Schiller J (2013). Active properties of neocortical pyramidal neuron dendrites. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*. PMID:23841837. [verified] — supports: Thin dendrites act as dynamic computational subunits; NMDA receptors provide the major nonlinear depolarizing drive up to full NMDA spikes, so a branch's input-output function is a clustered-input-triggered regenerative event set by network activity.
- Larkum M (2013). A cellular mechanism for cortical associations: an organizing principle for the cerebral cortex. *Trends in Neurosciences*. PMID:23273272. [verified] — supports: Coincident input to opposite poles of the pyramidal neuron (distal tuft + perisomatic) robustly triggers an associative dendritic Ca2+ spike mechanism (BAC firing), gated by compartment-targeted inhibition — the coincidence AND gate framing.
- Poirazi P, Brannon T, Mel BW (2003). Pyramidal neuron as two-layer neural network. *Neuron*. PMID:12670427. [verified] — supports: A pyramidal cell's firing rate is predicted by a two-layer model: independent sigmoidal subunits corresponding to long thin terminal dendrites, summed and thresholded at trunk/soma — the branch-as-subunit computational abstraction.
- Losonczy A, Makara JK, Magee JC (2008). Compartmentalized dendritic plasticity and input feature storage in neurons. *Nature*. PMID:18368112. [verified] — supports: Branch strength potentiation: dendritic spike-to-soma coupling is modified per-branch via NMDAR-dependent regulation of Kv4.2 K+ channels — information storage in branch excitability, mechanistically distinct from synaptic weight change.
- Cichon J, Gan WB (2015). Branch-specific dendritic Ca2+ spikes cause persistent synaptic plasticity. *Nature*. PMID:25822789. [verified] — supports: Different motor learning tasks induce Ca2+ spikes on different apical tuft branches in vivo; somatostatin interneurons enforce branch separation, and breaking it causes interference between learned tasks — branch-level memory allocation.
- Kole MH, Ilschner SU, Kampa BM, Williams SR, Ruben PC, Stuart GJ (2008). Action potential generation requires a high sodium channel density in the axon initial segment. *Nature Neuroscience*. PMID:18204443. [verified] — supports: The AIS carries ~50x the Na+ channel density of soma/proximal dendrites (~2500 pS/um^2), anchored to the actin cytoskeleton, and this density is required for action potential initiation — the physical basis of the decision threshold.
- Kole MH, Stuart GJ (2012). Signal processing in the axon initial segment. *Neuron*. PMID:22284179. [verified] — supports: The AIS is a dynamic signal-processing unit — clustered voltage-gated channels give it unique electrical properties that regulate synaptic integration, intrinsic excitability, and transmitter release, not just spike initiation.
- Grubb MS, Burrone J (2010). Activity-dependent relocation of the axon initial segment fine-tunes neuronal excitability. *Nature*. PMID:20543823. [verified] — supports: Chronic depolarization moves the entire AIS (channels and scaffold) up to 17 um distally in a reversible, Ca2+-channel-dependent manner, changing spike current threshold — homeostatic plasticity of the decision element on an hours-to-days timescale.
- Rasband MN (2010). The axon initial segment and the maintenance of neuronal polarity. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*. PMID:20631711. [verified] — supports: The AIS maintains neuronal polarity — ion-channel clustering plus a barrier/filter role that preserves axon-vs-dendrite identity throughout life, with disrupted polarity as a common disease component.
- Maday S, Twelvetrees AE, Moughamian AJ, Holzbaur EL (2014). Axonal transport: cargo-specific mechanisms of motility and regulation. *Neuron*. PMID:25374356. [verified] — supports: Anterograde transport supplies distal axons with newly synthesized proteins/lipids and synaptic components; retrograde transport removes aging proteins/organelles for degradation and carries neurotrophic and injury-response signaling back to the soma — cargo-specific bidirectional logistics.
- Luo L, O'Leary DD (2005). Axon retraction and degeneration in development and disease. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*. PMID:16022592. [verified] — supports: Selective elimination of axons, dendrites, branches, and synapses occurs WITHOUT loss of the parent neuron, via retraction or degeneration — compartment-scoped pruning as a normal developmental and plasticity mechanism.
- Coleman MP, Hoke A (2020). Programmed axon degeneration: from mouse to mechanism to medicine. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*. PMID:32152523. [verified] — supports: Wallerian degeneration is a locally executed, actively triggered axon-death program centered on loss of somally supplied labile NMNAT2 and activation of the NADase SARM1 — axon survival depends on continuous delivery from the soma, and the program runs independently of soma death.
