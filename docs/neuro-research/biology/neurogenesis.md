# neurogenesis: Adult and developmental neurogenesis and neuronal migration: niches, radial glia, migration, circuit integration, survival windows, and neurogenesis-dependent memory remodeling

## Summary
Based on articles retrieved from PubMed. New neurons are minted only at registered factories. In development, radial glial cells of the ventricular zone are simultaneously the progenitors and the routing infrastructure: they divide to produce neurons, and those neurons then climb their parent's radial fiber to their cortical address (Noctor 2001; Rakic 1972). In the adult, two niches persist: the subventricular zone (SVZ), where GFAP+ astrocyte-like B1 cells are the quiescent stem cells (Doetsch 1999), and the subgranular zone (SGZ) of the dentate gyrus. Both run the same pipeline: quiescent stem cell -> transit-amplifying progenitor (burst multiplier) -> migratory neuroblast -> immature neuron (Ming & Song 2011). SVZ neuroblasts travel millimeters in chains along one fixed highway, the rostral migratory stream, to the olfactory bulb, then switch from tangential to radial migration for local addressing (Lois & Alvarez-Buylla 1994); SGZ neuroblasts migrate only locally into the granule layer. Subtype identity is largely specified at the origin, not the destination.

Integration is a staged, signal-gated handshake. A newborn granule cell is first tonically depolarized by ambient GABA — because its intracellular chloride is high (NKCC1-dominant), GABA is excitatory; this lets the naive unit read aggregate network traffic before it has any specific synapses. The sequence is fixed: tonic GABA -> GABAergic synapses -> glutamatergic synapses -> output; forcing GABA to be inhibitory early (premature KCC2) wrecks dendritic growth and synapse formation (Ge 2006). Output wiring is verified end-to-end: adult-born cells form functional glutamatergic boutons on hilar interneurons, mossy cells, and CA3 pyramids (Toni 2008).

Survival is competitive and input-gated: use-it-or-lose-it. The system over-provisions, then selects. Most newborn cells die in days 1-4 (amplifying-progenitor -> neuroblast transition) and are silently cleared by unchallenged microglia via apoptosis-coupled phagocytosis (Sierra 2010). Cells passing that gate face a second checkpoint around weeks 1-3: single-cell NMDA-receptor knockout shows each cell's own coincidence-detecting glutamate receptor — i.e., evidence of correlated input onto that specific cell — determines whether it survives, cell-autonomously and competitively (Tashiro 2006). The death executor is the proapoptotic protein Bax; Bax knockout abolishes this programmed death and granule cells accumulate (Sun 2004). Experience tunes the threshold: enriched environments increase the number of surviving new neurons (Kempermann 1997). Survivors then get a transient privilege: at cell age 4-6 weeks they show a lowered LTP induction threshold and enhanced LTP amplitude, carried by NR2B-containing NMDA receptors — a rolling supply of temporarily high-learning-rate units that later anneal to the stability of the old cohort (Ge 2007).

Neurogenesis rewrites old memory. Ablating adult neurogenesis impairs fine spatial pattern separation — forming orthogonal codes for similar inputs (Clelland 2009). Conversely, boosting neurogenesis after learning degrades established hippocampal memories, and high infant neurogenesis explains infantile amnesia; suppressing it preserves infant memories (Akers 2014). Growth and forgetting are one mechanism: adding units remodels existing connectivity, clearing capacity while old memories are presumed consolidated elsewhere (cortex).

Timescales: progenitor cycle ~days; RMS transit ~days-2 weeks; apoptosis window days 1-4; NMDA survival window weeks 1-3; GABA polarity switch ~weeks 2-4; enhanced-plasticity critical period weeks 4-6; full maturation ~2 months.

Human caveat: adult human hippocampal neurogenesis was established by BrdU labeling (Eriksson 1998) but is now contested — one careful study finds it undetectable in adults (Sorrells 2018), another finds it persisting through old age (Boldrini 2018). Even in biology, mature systems may throttle unit-addition to near zero while keeping the machinery.

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Niche-restricted stem cell activation (SVZ B1 astrocytes, SGZ radial-glia-like cells)**: Signal: niche factors (vascular contact, ependymal signals, Notch/BMP, ambient GABA) hold GFAP+ stem cells quiescent; activation is the exception. Pipeline: stem cell -> transit-amplifying progenitor (burst multiplier, ~days per cycle) -> neuroblast. Threshold: admission control — new units are only minted at two registered factories, never ad hoc in the field. Timescale: continuous low-rate production, modulated by systemic state (exercise, stress, age).
- **Radial glia as lineage-parent-and-scaffold (radial unit)**: The progenitor that creates a neuron is also its routing path: the daughter migrates along the parent's radial fiber, so spatial address is inherited from parent topology (parent = union/anchor of children). Signal: fiber adhesion for locomotion; stop signal (reelin at the marginal zone) terminates migration. Timescale: days per neuron; inside-out layering means arrival time encodes layer identity.
- **Chain migration along the rostral migratory stream, then tangential-to-radial switch**: Bulk transport on one fixed highway: neuroblasts slide along each other in glial-ensheathed chains over millimeters (days to ~2 weeks), carrying origin-specified identity. On arrival a mode switch (tangential -> radial) performs local addressing. Computationally: routing = fixed trunk line + last-mile dispersal; the item's identity is set at source, its final coordinates at destination.
- **GABA polarity switch as staged integration handshake**: Signal: ambient (tonic) GABA reports aggregate network activity to the synapse-less newcomer. Tag: intracellular chloride setpoint (NKCC1-high) makes GABA depolarizing, so the global 'inhibition' broadcast reads as growth-promoting excitation to new units only. The switch to KCC2 (weeks 2-4) flips the sign — graduation from stimulation-seeking probationer to regulated member. Order is mandatory: tonic listening -> GABA synapses -> glutamate synapses -> output; skipping stages breaks wiring.
- **Competitive input-gated survival checkpoint (use-it-or-lose-it) with silent garbage collection**: Over-provision then select. Default path is death: most newborn cells apoptose at days 1-4; survivors face a weeks-1-3 gate where each cell's own NMDA receptor (a coincidence detector: input activity AND own depolarization) must fire or the cell loses the competition. Executor: Bax-dependent apoptosis (delete verb); collector: microglial apoptosis-coupled phagocytosis — immediate, non-inflammatory, undeterred by load. Threshold is experience-tuned: enriched environment lowers the bar, sparing more units. Retention requires evidence of use inside a fixed probation window.
- **Critical period of enhanced plasticity in surviving new units**: Tag: NR2B-containing NMDA receptors, developmentally expressed at cell age 4-6 weeks. Effect: lower LTP induction threshold + higher LTP amplitude — a temporarily elevated learning rate that makes the newest cohort the preferential encoder of current experience. Timescale: transient; the privilege decays with maturation, so the network gets a rolling supply of plastic units without destabilizing tenured ones (stability-plasticity solved by staggered unit age).
- **Verified bidirectional circuit insertion (output synaptogenesis onto existing targets)**: Integration is not complete at input acceptance: the new granule cell must also form functional glutamatergic output synapses onto hilar interneurons, mossy cells, and CA3 pyramids, partly competing for existing synaptic territory. Computationally: a join is closed only when the new node both receives and successfully delivers on live routes — a confirming round-trip, at ~4-8 weeks cell age.
- **Neurogenesis-coupled forgetting and pattern separation (growth = overwrite)**: Adding units physically remodels dentate connectivity: higher neurogenesis after encoding degrades stored hippocampal memories (infantile amnesia is the extreme case); blocking it preserves them. The same addition provides sparse orthogonal codes for similar inputs (pattern separation). Logic: capacity for new distinct representations is purchased by controlled decay of old episodic detail — forgetting is an active byproduct of growth, not passive rot, and it presumes older memories were consolidated into cortex first.

## Citations
- Doetsch F, Caillé I, Lim DA, García-Verdugo JM, Alvarez-Buylla A (1999). Subventricular zone astrocytes are neural stem cells in the adult mammalian brain. *Cell*. PMID:10380923. [verified] — supports: GFAP+ astrocyte-like B1 cells of the adult SVZ are the neural stem cells that generate transit-amplifying precursors, neuroblasts, and ultimately new olfactory bulb neurons.
- Noctor SC, Flint AC, Weissman TA, Dammerman RS, Kriegstein AR (2001). Neurons derived from radial glial cells establish radial units in neocortex. *Nature*. PMID:11217860. [verified] — supports: Radial glia are neuronal progenitors in vivo, and newborn neurons migrate along the radial fibers of their own clonally related radial glia, making the lineage parent also the migration scaffold (radial unit).
- Rakic P (1972). Mode of cell migration to the superficial layers of fetal monkey neocortex. *Journal of Comparative Neurology*. PMID:4624784. [verified] — supports: Classic EM study establishing that migrating cortical neurons are guided along radial glial fibers from the ventricular zone to their cortical destination (glia-guided locomotion).
- Lois C, Alvarez-Buylla A (1994). Long-distance neuronal migration in the adult mammalian brain. *Science*. PMID:8178174. [verified] — supports: Adult SVZ cells migrate long distances (the rostral migratory stream route) and differentiate into olfactory bulb neurons, demonstrating targeted long-range neuroblast migration in the adult brain.
- Eriksson PS, Perfilieva E, Björk-Eriksson T, Alborn AM, Nordborg C, Peterson DA, Gage FH (1998). Neurogenesis in the adult human hippocampus. *Nature Medicine*. PMID:9809557. [verified] — supports: BrdU-labeled dividing progenitors generate new NeuN+ neurons in the adult human dentate gyrus, the foundational evidence for lifelong human hippocampal neurogenesis.
- Ming GL, Song H (2011). Adult neurogenesis in the mammalian brain: significant answers and significant questions. *Neuron*. PMID:21609825. [verified] — supports: Modern review consolidating the SGZ and SVZ->RMS->olfactory bulb pipelines, the stem cell -> amplifying progenitor -> neuroblast -> mature neuron stages, niche regulation, and integration timelines.
- Ge S, Goh EL, Sailor KA, Kitabatake Y, Ming GL, Song H (2006). GABA regulates synaptic integration of newly generated neurons in the adult brain. *Nature*. PMID:16341203. [verified] — supports: Newborn granule cells are first tonically activated by ambient GABA, which is depolarizing due to high intracellular chloride; integration follows a fixed GABA-then-glutamate sequence, and converting GABA's action to hyperpolarizing early causes marked defects in synapse formation and dendritic development.
- Ge S, Yang CH, Hsu KS, Ming GL, Song H (2007). A critical period for enhanced synaptic plasticity in newly generated neurons of the adult brain. *Neuron*. PMID:17521569. [verified] — supports: Adult-born granule cells at 1-1.5 months of age show enhanced LTP amplitude and lowered induction threshold, dependent on NR2B-containing NMDA receptors — a transient critical period of elevated plasticity.
- Tashiro A, Sandler VM, Toni N, Zhao C, Gage FH (2006). NMDA-receptor-mediated, cell-specific integration of new neurons in adult dentate gyrus. *Nature*. PMID:16906136. [verified] — supports: Single-cell NMDA receptor knockout shows survival of new neurons is competitively regulated by each cell's own input activity during a short critical period after birth — the input-gated, use-it-or-lose-it survival checkpoint.
- Sun W, Winseck A, Vinsant S, Park OH, Kim H, Oppenheim RW (2004). Programmed cell death of adult-generated hippocampal neurons is mediated by the proapoptotic gene Bax. *Journal of Neuroscience*. PMID:15590937. [verified] — supports: The programmed death of adult-generated dentate neurons is executed by Bax; Bax deletion abolishes this death and granule neurons accumulate, showing apoptosis is the default fate operating during early differentiation.
- Sierra A, Encinas JM, Deudero JJ, Chancey JH, Enikolopov G, Overstreet-Wadiche LS, Tsirka SE, Maletic-Savatic M (2010). Microglia shape adult hippocampal neurogenesis through apoptosis-coupled phagocytosis. *Cell Stem Cell*. PMID:20887954. [verified] — supports: The majority of newborn SGZ cells die by apoptosis in days 1-4 of life (amplifying progenitor to neuroblast transition) and are rapidly and efficiently cleared by unchallenged resident microglia.
- Kempermann G, Kuhn HG, Gage FH (1997). More hippocampal neurons in adult mice living in an enriched environment. *Nature*. PMID:9087407. [verified] — supports: Environmental enrichment increases the number of surviving new dentate granule neurons, demonstrating experience-dependent regulation of newborn neuron survival.
- Toni N, Laplagne DA, Zhao C, Lombardi G, Ribak CE, Gage FH, Schinder AF (2008). Neurons born in the adult dentate gyrus form functional synapses with target cells. *Nature Neuroscience*. PMID:18622400. [verified] — supports: Adult-born granule cells form functional glutamatergic output synapses onto hilar interneurons, mossy cells, and CA3 pyramidal cells, completing bidirectional integration into the pre-existing circuit.
- Clelland CD, Choi M, Romberg C, Clemenson GD, Fragniere A, Tyers P, Jessberger S, Saksida LM, Barker RA, Gage FH, Bussey TJ (2009). A functional role for adult hippocampal neurogenesis in spatial pattern separation. *Science*. PMID:19590004. [verified] — supports: Ablating adult hippocampal neurogenesis selectively impairs discrimination of closely spaced (highly similar) spatial stimuli, showing newborn neurons are required for fine pattern separation in the dentate gyrus.
- Akers KG, Martinez-Canabal A, Restivo L, et al., Frankland PW (2014). Hippocampal neurogenesis regulates forgetting during adulthood and infancy. *Science*. PMID:24812394. [verified] — supports: Increasing neurogenesis after memory formation induces forgetting of established hippocampal memories; high infant neurogenesis underlies infantile amnesia, and suppressing it mitigates forgetting — continuous unit addition actively remodels and degrades stored circuits.
- Sorrells SF, Paredes MF, Cebrian-Silla A, et al., Alvarez-Buylla A (2018). Human hippocampal neurogenesis drops sharply in children to undetectable levels in adults. *Nature*. PMID:29513649. [verified] — supports: In carefully staged human post-mortem and surgical tissue, proliferating dentate progenitors and young neurons decline sharply in childhood and are undetectable in adults — the strongest evidence that adult human hippocampal neurogenesis is rare or absent.
- Boldrini M, Fulmore CA, Tartt AN, et al., Mann JJ (2018). Human Hippocampal Neurogenesis Persists throughout Aging. *Cell Stem Cell*. PMID:29625071. [verified] — supports: In healthy human autopsy hippocampi aged 14-79, similar numbers of intermediate progenitors and thousands of immature neurons are found across ages — the counter-evidence that human hippocampal neurogenesis persists lifelong (with reduced quiescent pool and angiogenesis in aging).
