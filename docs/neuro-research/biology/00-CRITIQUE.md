# Verification critic: corrections and additions to the literature sweep

## Thin areas / corrections (respect these; do not repeat flagged misattributions)
- growth-cones / topographic addressing is built on a citation that does not support it. The sweep's 'address computed from relative, not absolute, values, so the map rescales automatically' is attributed to Drescher 1995 (PMID 7634326), but that paper's own abstract states RAGS repelled 'axons of temporal as well as nasal origin, indicating that an additional activity may be necessary to confer the nasotemporal specificity' — i.e. it explicitly failed to show a receptor-scaled threshold. Two canonical results must be added: Cheng & Flanagan 1995 (PMID 7634327, 'Complementary gradients in expression and binding of ELF-1 and Mek4') for the matched reciprocal gradients, and Reber, Burrola & Lemke 2004 (PMID 15483613, 'A relative signalling model for the formation of a topographic neural map') which is the actual demonstration that mapping uses RATIOS of EphA signalling rather than absolute differences — the precise property the mapping wants.
- hebbian-ltp-ltd is missing behavioral-timescale synaptic plasticity, and this is the largest single gap in the sweep. The timing story stops at the STDP +/-20 ms window. Bittner et al. 2017 (PMID 28883072, 'Behavioral time scale synaptic plasticity underlies CA1 place fields') shows one-trial place-field formation by potentiation of input arriving SECONDS before and after a dendritic Ca plateau, input that was 'neither causal nor close in time to postsynaptic activation.' A memory system assigning credit across a multi-second episode has no biological anchor without it; STDP's millisecond window actively misleads here.
- hebbian-ltp-ltd defines weight as AMPA-receptor count with no structural channel. Missing Matsuzaki et al. 2004 (PMID 15190253, 'Structural basis of long-term potentiation in single dendritic spines') — spine-head enlargement as the structural expression of LTP, with volume tracking strength. This is the substrate that makes the two-regime retention result already cited in synaptogenesis (Holtmaat 2005: thin spines days, thick spines months) mechanistically coherent; without it, 'weight' and 'durability' are unlinked across areas.
- The maintenance problem — how a stored value survives complete turnover of the molecules holding it — is never posed in any area, and the one molecule invoked for it is contested. tagging-capture presents PKMzeta as a PRP that 'maintains potentiation' (Sajikumar 2005) without Volk et al. 2013 (PMID 23283174, 'PKM-zeta is not required for hippocampal synaptic plasticity, learning and memory'), which reports normal LTP and normal hippocampal memory in PKC-zeta/PKM-zeta knockouts AND that ZIP still reverses LTP in those knockouts, invalidating the pharmacology the original claim rests on. Either cite the rebuttal or drop the reliance; also missing any self-perpetuating-state mechanism (CaMKII subunit exchange, CPEB/Orb2 prion-like states).
- engrams lists 'Reactivation-as-retrieval via pattern completion' as a mechanism but cites no circuit result for the computation. Must cite Nakazawa et al. 2002 (PMID 12040087) — CA3-restricted NR1 knockout leaves full-cue recall intact but abolishes recall from PARTIAL cues, the definitive completion result — and Leutgeb et al. 2007 (PMID 17303747), which shows DG separating while CA3 completes across the same graded input morph. This is the retrieval-key mechanism the whole mapping depends on and it is currently asserted, not sourced.
- engrams discusses two load-bearing results by name that appear in no citation record: Ramirez 2013 (pairing artificial reactivation with shock installs a false memory) and Zhou 2009 (CREB acts on allocation via intrinsic excitability). The excitability-mediation step is the causal link in the entire allocation story and it is currently unanchored.
- neurogenesis cites only the necessity half of the pattern-separation claim. Clelland 2009 (PMID 19590004) ablates neurogenesis and finds impairment; the sufficiency counterpart is Sahay et al. 2011 (PMID 21460835), where enhancing survival of adult-born granule cells improves pattern separation without disturbing other hippocampal functions. 'Adding units improves separation' cannot rest on an ablation result alone.
- sleep-consolidation treats replay as sleep-only and therefore misses the entire online consolidation mode. Foster & Wilson 2006 (PMID 16474382) established reverse replay during awake rest, and Jadhav et al. 2012 (PMID 22555434) selectively disrupted AWAKE sharp-wave ripples and impaired spatial learning. For the mapping this is the difference between 'compaction runs only in a nightly batch window' and 'compaction runs during idle moments between requests' — architecturally a different system.
- sleep-consolidation's account of sleep is purely subtractive (de Vivo downscaling). Missing Yang et al. 2014 (PMID 24904169, 'Sleep promotes branch-specific formation of dendritic spines after learning'), which shows sleep also BUILDS, and does so branch-addressably — directly connecting to the branch-level allocation mechanism in neuron-anatomy and the clustered capture in tagging-capture. Also missing REM entirely: Boyce et al. 2016 (PMID 27174984) shows optogenetic disruption of REM theta impairs consolidation, so the single-stage SWS story is incomplete.
- astrocytes states D-serine gating of the plasticity threshold as settled, but the cited paper's own abstract flags the opposite: Henneberger 2010 (PMID 20075918) notes that 'loading an astrocyte with exogenous Ca2+ buffers does not suppress LTP in hippocampal area CA1' in other hands and that 'the involvement of glia in LTP induction therefore remains controversial.' The IP3R2-knockout counter-literature (Petravicz 2008, PMID 18463250; Agulhon 2010, PMID 20203048), which finds intact synaptic transmission and plasticity without astrocytic Ca2+ signalling, must be cited or the claim hedged — otherwise a contested gate is mapped as a hard architectural dependency.
- tagging-capture presents a theoretical model as an empirical refinement. Govindarajan, Kelleher & Tonegawa 2006 (PMID 16791146) is a Nature Reviews Neuroscience model paper ('A clustered plasticity model of long-term memory engrams'), not a demonstration that tagged synapses on a shared branch win competition for limited PRPs. The experimental result is Govindarajan et al. 2011, Neuron (PMID 21220104), 'The dendritic branch is the preferred integrative unit for protein synthesis-dependent LTP.'
- Inhibition is effectively absent across the whole sweep. 'Lateral inhibition enforcing sparsity' (engrams) and one somatostatin mention (neuron-anatomy) are the entirety of it, and neither is cited. There are no interneuron classes, no E/I balance, and no divisive normalization (Carandini & Heeger 2012, PMID 22108672, 'Normalization as a canonical neural computation'). Sparsity enforcement, capacity ceilings and read arbitration in the mapped memory system will have no biological grounding.
- growth-cones is missing axon-autonomous local translation. Campbell & Holt 2001 (PMID 11754834) showed netrin- and Sema3A-induced turning and collapse require local protein synthesis and degradation within the growth cone itself, decoupled from the soma. This is the mechanism by which a remote actuator computes without a round trip to the central factory — directly relevant to the neuron-anatomy area's 'metered logistics' framing, and it belongs in both.
- Neither synaptogenesis nor microglia-pruning covers self-versus-non-self discrimination or duplicate-link avoidance. Clustered protocadherin self-avoidance (Lefebvre et al. 2012, PMID 22842903, 'Protocadherins mediate dendritic self-avoidance in the mammalian nervous system') is the canonical result: a combinatorial single-cell identity code that makes a process repel its own siblings but not neighbours. A memory system that grows links needs exactly this — a mechanism preventing self-referential and redundant edges — and nothing in the sweep supplies it.

## Suspect citations (use the corrected sources; soften contested claims)
- Drescher U, Kremoser C, Handwerker C, Loschinger J, Noda M, Bonhoeffer F 1995 (PMID:7634326) — VERIFIED RECORD, MISATTRIBUTED CLAIM. Record is correct (Cell 82(3):359-70). But the sweep uses it for 'each axon stops where repulsion crosses its own receptor-scaled threshold — an address computed from relative, not absolute, values, so the map rescales automatically.' The paper reports the opposite of the specificity half: RAGS repelled temporal AND nasal axons, and the authors state an additional activity is needed for nasotemporal specificity. The relative/ratio result is Reber 2004 (PMID 15483613); the matched-gradient result is Cheng & Flanagan 1995 (PMID 7634327).
- 'RAGS/ephrin-A2' (growth-cones prose) — PROBABLE NAMING ERROR. RAGS was subsequently identified as ephrin-A5; ephrin-A2 is ELF-1, from Cheng & Flanagan 1995 (PMID 7634327), a different paper in the same Cell issue. Note that NLM indexes BOTH papers under the MeSH term 'Ephrin-A2', so PubMed metadata will not resolve this — it needs a human check against current nomenclature before the mapping inherits the wrong ligand name.
- Sajikumar S, Navakkode S, Sacktor TC, Frey JU 2005 (PMID:15958741) — RECORD FINE, CLAIM NOW CONTESTED. The sweep states as fact that PKMzeta 'maintains potentiation.' Volk et al. 2013 (PMID 23283174) found normal LTP and normal hippocampal learning/memory in PKC-zeta/PKM-zeta knockouts, and critically that ZIP — the inhibitor all the original evidence relies on — still reverses LTP in those knockouts, meaning ZIP's effects are PKMzeta-independent. Flag as unsupported-as-stated rather than as a citation error.
- Govindarajan A, Kelleher RJ, Tonegawa S 2006 (PMID:16791146) — CORRECT RECORD, WRONG EVIDENCE CLASS. Verified as Nat Rev Neurosci 7(7):575-83, article type Review; it proposes the clustered plasticity model. The sweep cites it for an empirical finding about branch-level capture competition and time-clustered co-allocation. Substitute or add Govindarajan et al. 2011, Neuron 69(1):132 (PMID 21220104).
- Frey U, Morris RG 1998, TINS (PMID:9610879) — REVIEW CITED AS PRIMARY SOURCE. Verified correct (Trends Neurosci 21(5):181-8, article type Review). It is cited for the requirement of dopamine D1/D5 activation for PRP synthesis; the abstract only says it 'outline[s] the requirement for aminergic activation.' The primary D1/D5 late-LTP results (Frey et al. 1990/1991; Huang & Kandel 1995) are not in the citation list, so the neuromodulatory gate — a load-bearing element of the design mapping — has no primary source.
- Mitchison T, Kirschner M 1988 (PMID:3078414) — REVIEW-ONLY SOURCING FOR A LOAD-BEARING MECHANISM. Verified correct (Neuron 1(9):761-72) but article type is Review, as is the co-cited Lowery & Van Vactor 2009. The molecular clutch is the central actuator mechanism in growth-cones and no primary measurement of clutch engagement or of the flow-versus-traction relationship is cited.
- Weinhard L et al. 2018 (PMID:29581545) — INFERENCE FROM A NULL RESULT. Record verified (Nat Commun 9(1):1228). The quoted trogocytosis statistics (2.3 +/- 0.7 vs 1.5 +/- 0.6 events/cell/3 h, p = 0.37) are not in the abstract and could not be confirmed here; more importantly, a non-significant difference is being used to assert that trogocytosis is 'a pathway separate from complement-tagged engulfment.' That is absence of evidence, not evidence of separation — the two-pathway architecture claim should be softened.
- engrams area — TWO UNCITED RESULTS. 'Ramirez 2013' (false memory installation) and 'Zhou 2009' (excitability as the mediating variable for CREB-driven allocation) are named in the prose but appear in no citation record, so neither PMID, year, nor journal can be checked and the claims are unverifiable as written.
- Dudek SM, Bear MF 1992 (PMID:1350090) — MINOR PARAMETER IMPRECISION. The abstract gives 'nine hundred pulses at 1-3 Hz'; the sweep renders this as '900 pulses at 1-3 Hz for ~15 min.' 900 pulses take 15 min only at 1 Hz (5 min at 3 Hz). Trivial, but the sweep presents these as exact induction parameters.
- VERIFICATION SCOPE: approximately 50 of the ~130 cited PMIDs were fetched directly from PubMed, selected for those carrying precise quantitative or attributional claims (Lehre & Danbolt transporter densities, Kole AIS conductance, Grubb 17 um AIS shift, Holtmaat 35%/73% persistence, Xiao 2.5 h/4 h oligodendrogenesis, de Vivo 6,920 synapses/~18%, Roy 247/117 regions, Friedman 30/45 min assembly, Lee 1:2:4 stoichiometry, Ge 2007 critical period, Frey & Morris tag decay windows, Luo 10 pM collapsin, Ziv & Smith 9.5 min filopodia, Wake ~5 min/~1 h contacts, Sierra days 1-4, and others). Every one resolved to the correct paper, year, journal and — where checkable against the abstract — the correct numbers. Apart from the Drescher misattribution above, no PMID/year/journal mismatches were found; the sweep's citation hygiene is otherwise strong.

## Missing cross-cutting mechanisms (added by critic, with key citations — usable as evidence)
- **Reconsolidation: retrieval destabilizes a stored item and it must be re-committed** — The single largest cross-cutting omission. Every area treats read and write as separate operations, but in biology a successful read puts the record back into a labile state where it must be re-written to persist — a reactivated memory becomes protein-synthesis-dependent again, and blocking synthesis during that window erases it while the same block outside the window does nothing. For a software memory system this is the difference between an idempotent read and a read that opens a transaction with a bounded commit window: it supplies update-on-access, drift with each retrieval, and a principled point at which a stored item can be edited rather than appended to. The sweep has retrieval (engrams), consolidation (sleep, tagging) and erasure (forgetting) but nothing connecting reading to re-writing. KEY CITATION: Nader K, Schafe GE, LeDoux JE 2000, Nature 406:722-6 (PMID:10963596) — 'Fear memories require protein synthesis in the amygdala for reconsolidation after retrieval'
- **Prediction error as the gate on destabilization and update** — The necessary companion to reconsolidation, and the mechanism that keeps it from being pathological. Retrieval alone does NOT destabilize a memory; a mismatch between what was expected and what occurred is what opens the update window. Without it, reconsolidation maps to 'every read rewrites,' which is both wrong and expensive. With it, the rule is 'a read that confirms the record leaves it alone; a read that contradicts it opens the record for edit' — a directly implementable invalidation policy that no area in the sweep supplies. KEY CITATION: Sevenster D, Beckers T, Kindt M 2013, Science 339:830-3 (PMID:23413355) — 'Prediction error governs pharmacologically induced amnesia for learned fear'
- **Pattern separation and pattern completion as an explicit circuit computation** — The sweep asserts both — 'fine spatial pattern separation' in neurogenesis, 'reactivation-as-retrieval via pattern completion' in engrams — as named mechanisms without citing the circuit results that establish either. This is the read/write key mechanism for the whole mapping: separation is how similar inputs get non-colliding keys (the hash), completion is how a partial cue retrieves a whole record (the lookup). Nakazawa's CA3-NR1 knockout is the clean dissociation, since full-cue recall stays intact while partial-cue recall fails; Leutgeb shows DG and CA3 doing opposite transforms on the same graded input, which is the two-stage index architecture. KEY CITATION: Nakazawa K et al. 2002, Science 297:211-8 (PMID:12040087) — 'Requirement for hippocampal CA3 NMDA receptors in associative memory recall'; with Leutgeb JK et al. 2007, Science 315:961-6 (PMID:17303747)
- **Neuromodulatory salience scoring: dopamine reward-prediction-error and locus-coeruleus novelty as the write-priority signal** — The sweep contains exactly one neuromodulatory gate (D1/D5 on PRP synthesis in tagging-capture) and no account of what computes salience in the first place. Dopamine neurons emit a prediction-error signal — firing to unexpected reward, going silent to omitted expected reward — which is the biological answer to 'which events deserve durable storage,' and Takeuchi showed the locus-coeruleus dopaminergic input is what converts an ordinary everyday memory into a persistent one. This is the write-priority scorer the mapped system needs, and without it the tagging area's 'salience gate' is a gate with no upstream input. KEY CITATION: Schultz W, Dayan P, Montague PR 1997, Science 275:1593-9 (PMID:9054347) — 'A neural substrate of prediction and reward'; with Takeuchi T et al. 2016, Nature 537:357-62 (PMID:27602521)
- **Schema-dependent fast consolidation: prior structure collapses the commit cost** — Every consolidation story in the sweep (CLS, sleep replay, systems consolidation in engrams) assumes a fixed slow rate — weeks of interleaved replay before cortical independence. Tse showed this is conditional, not architectural: once an associative schema exists in neocortex, a single trial of consistent new information becomes hippocampus-independent in about 48 hours. This is the most directly actionable finding in the whole space for a memory system — writing into an existing structure is orders of magnitude cheaper than a cold write — and it also predicts the failure mode, where schema-inconsistent items stay expensive and stay dependent on the fast store. KEY CITATION: Tse D, Langston RF, Kakeyama M, Bethus I, Spooner PA, Wood ER, Witter MP, Morris RGM 2007, Science 316:76-82 (PMID:17412951) — 'Schemas and memory consolidation'
- **Awake replay and offline-but-not-asleep consolidation** — sleep-consolidation is framed entirely as a nightly batch process, which mis-specifies the mapping. Sharp-wave ripples during quiet waking replay just-experienced sequences in reverse, and selectively disrupting AWAKE ripples — leaving sleep ripples intact — impairs spatial learning. The system therefore runs consolidation opportunistically during idle moments between episodes, not only in a dedicated offline window. For a memory system this changes the scheduler from a cron job to an idle-time background task, and adds reverse-order replay, which is the credit-assignment direction. KEY CITATION: Jadhav SP, Kemere C, German PW, Frank LM 2012, Science 336:1454-8 (PMID:22555434) — 'Awake hippocampal sharp-wave ripples support spatial memory'; with Foster DJ, Wilson MA 2006, Nature 440:680-3 (PMID:16474382)
- **Behavioral-timescale eligibility traces (seconds-scale credit assignment)** — Listed here as well as under thin areas because it is genuinely cross-cutting: tagging-capture has an hours-scale eligibility trace, hebbian-ltp-ltd has a 20 ms one, and there is nothing at the seconds scale where actual episodes happen. Bittner's rule potentiates input that arrived seconds before AND after the plateau — non-causal, order-symmetric, one-trial — which is exactly the regime a memory system operates in when deciding which of the last several seconds of context belongs to an event worth storing. KEY CITATION: Bittner KC, Milstein AD, Grienberger C, Romani S, Magee JC 2017, Science 357:1033-6 (PMID:28883072) — 'Behavioral time scale synaptic plasticity underlies CA1 place fields'
- **Index-versus-content separation (hippocampal indexing theory)** — engrams gets close with the connectivity-versus-weights split and the silent engram, but never states the architectural claim: the hippocampus stores a compact INDEX of the cortical pattern, not the pattern itself, and retrieval is index-driven reinstatement of distributed cortical content. This is the cleanest available biological warrant for separating a small fast index from large slow content stores — including the corollary that the index can be intact while content is unreachable, and that index and content can be aged out independently. KEY CITATION: Teyler TJ, Rudy JW 2007, Hippocampus 17:1158-69 (PMID:17696170) — 'The hippocampal indexing theory and episodic memory: updating the index'
- **Representational drift: stable behavior over a changing code** — Nothing in the sweep addresses the fact that the neurons representing a given memory turn over across days while the behavior stays constant — the code drifts even as the function persists. For a memory system built on embeddings or learned keys this is the most consequential unmodeled phenomenon of all: it means stored keys go stale relative to the encoder, and any design that assumes a retrieval key stays valid indefinitely is assuming something biology does not do. It also constrains how the sweep's other mechanisms are read, since 'the same engram' is partly a moving target. KEY CITATION: Ziv Y, Burns LD, Cocker ED, Hamel EO, Ghosh KK, Kitch LJ, El Gamal A, Schnitzer MJ 2013, Nat Neurosci 16:264-6 (PMID:23396101) — 'Long-term dynamics of CA1 hippocampal place codes'
- **Divisive normalization and inhibitory gain control** — The sweep's only stabilizing mechanisms are slow ones — BCM sliding threshold and 24-48 h synaptic scaling. Divisive normalization is the fast, always-on counterpart: a unit's response is divided by summed neighbourhood activity, which enforces sparsity, bounds dynamic range, and implements competition among simultaneously active items on a millisecond timescale. It is the mechanism behind the 'lateral inhibition enforcing sparsity' that engrams asserts without citation, and it is the read-time arbitration rule for which item wins when several match. KEY CITATION: Carandini M, Heeger DJ 2012, Nat Rev Neurosci 13:51-62 (PMID:22108672) — 'Normalization as a canonical neural computation'
- **Extinction as a competing new record rather than deletion (context-gated retrieval)** — forgetting-apoptosis covers decay, active erasure, and accessibility switching, but not the case where the old record survives intact and is merely out-competed by a newer, context-keyed one — proven by renewal (the old response returns outside the extinction context), spontaneous recovery over time, and reinstatement. This is precisely the tombstone/shadowing pattern in a versioned store: the superseding write is context-scoped, the superseded record is still there, and retrieval context decides which one is returned. It also explains why the sweep's own GluA2-3Y result blocks spontaneous recovery, which currently sits unexplained in that area. KEY CITATION: Bouton ME 2004, Learn Mem 11:485-94 (PMID:15466298) — 'Context and behavioral processes in extinction'
