# sleep-consolidation: Sleep replay and systems consolidation: hippocampal sharp-wave ripples, the hippocampo-neocortical dialogue of slow-wave sleep, active systems consolidation (Born/Diekelmann), the synaptic homeostasis hypothesis (Tononi/Cirelli), targeted memory reactivation, and complementary learning systems (McClelland)

## Summary
TWO STORES, TWO LEARNING RATES. Complementary Learning Systems (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995) is an architectural argument, not a biological observation: neocortex must learn *slowly* and *interleaved* to discover structure across ensembles of experiences; a fast writer into those same weights causes catastrophic interference. Hippocampus is therefore a fast, sparse, one-shot writer whose codes barely overlap. The load-bearing consequence: a fast store is only safe if an offline process re-presents its contents to the slow store, interleaved with other content. Replay is that re-presentation. Remote memory is the accumulated residue of many small neocortical changes, one per reinstatement.

THE PACKET. Wilson & McNaughton (1994) established the signal: place-cell pairs co-active during behavior show elevated co-firing in subsequent slow-wave sleep relative to pre-behavior baseline; cells that were active but non-overlapping do not; and the bias *decays gradually across the sleep session*. Replay is a decaying, experience-tagged firing bias — not a stored copy. Its carrier is the sharp-wave ripple (SPW-R), the most synchronous population event in the mammalian brain (Buzsáki 2015): CA3 recurrent excitation drives a fast CA1 network oscillation lasting tens to a couple hundred milliseconds, its spike content temporally sculpted by interneurons so that waking sequences are re-emitted *in compressed format*. Compression is the whole trick: an episode that took seconds is re-emitted inside one plasticity-eligible burst.

CAUSALITY AND THRESHOLD. Girardeau et al. (2009) ran closed-loop online SPW-R detection and selectively aborted ripples during post-training rest — spatial memory was impaired. Replay is necessary. Fernández-Ruiz et al. (2019) found the graded control variable is ripple *duration*: durations are skewed, memory demand lengthens them, and optogenetically prolonging spontaneously initiated ripples improved maze learning — while randomly *inducing* ripples of the same shape did not, because induced events carried little spatial information. Shape without payload buys nothing; prolonging a genuine event recruits more of the route.

THE TRANSPORT FRAME. Staresina et al. (2015), human intracranial recordings, showed a strict three-level nesting: the neocortical slow oscillation's up-state phase-modulates thalamocortical spindles, and spindle troughs in turn cluster hippocampal ripples. A hierarchical clock — the SO up-state opens a depolarized cortical window, the spindle subdivides it into slots, ripples are delivered into those slots. Maingret et al. (2016) proved it causal in the enhancing direction: on a task engineered to encode but not consolidate, timed stimulation that reinforced endogenous SPW-R → cortical delta → spindle coordination reorganized prefrontal networks and produced next-day recall, where controls performed at chance. Diekelmann & Born (2010) add the neuromodulatory gate: this redistribution happens at *minimum cholinergic tone* — the low-ACh state is what permits hippocampal output to drive cortex rather than the reverse.

ADDRESSING. Replay is not a blind flush. Rothschild, Eban & Frank (2017) found cortical activity *precedes and predicts* the content of the next ripple, and the ripple predicts subsequent cortical activity — a cortical→hippocampal→cortical loop in which cortex effectively issues a query and hippocampus returns a matching episode. This channel is externally addressable: Rasch et al. (2007) re-presented a learning-context odor during SWS (ineffective in REM, in wake, or if the odor was absent at learning) and selectively improved hippocampus-dependent declarative — not procedural — memory, with fMRI hippocampal activation. Targeted memory reactivation is a cue-keyed replay API keyed on the encoding context tag.

DOWNSCALING. SHY (Tononi & Cirelli 2014): wake is net potentiation, which costs energy and supplies, degrades signal-to-noise, and saturates learning; sleep renormalizes net synaptic strength, and activity-dependent *down-selection* is itself what yields consolidation and integration. de Vivo et al. (2017) measured it: 3D EM of 6,920 synapses in mouse cortex; axon-spine interface fell ~18% after sleep, proportionally to size (multiplicative scaling), but sparing large synapses and those containing recycling endosomes — a two-population architecture, ~80% weak/plastic and decaying, ~20% strong/stable and protected.

THE SYNTHESIS AND THE PRODUCT. Klinzing, Niethard & Born (2019) fuse the two theories: active systems consolidation runs *embedded within* global synaptic downscaling. Replay selectively re-potentiates what fires; uniform proportional decay erodes everything else; the surviving *ratio*, not any absolute weight, is the memory. Nothing is individually judged for deletion. And the output is not a copy: Durrant et al. (2011) showed overnight and nap consolidation improved recognition of *novel* sequences obeying the same probabilistic structure, with improvement correlating to the amount of SWS obtained. Klinzing et al. state it directly — consolidation yields abstracted, gist-like representations. Replay is lossy by design. Timescales: encode (ms–s) → ripple (50–200 ms events, hours post-experience, decaying) → nightly SWS cycles (nights–weeks) → cortical independence (weeks–years).

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Complementary two-rate stores with an interference-safe write path (CLS)**: Two stores with deliberately different learning rates and representational overlap. Fast store (hippocampus): sparse, pattern-separated, one-shot write, high capacity for specifics, low interference because codes barely overlap. Slow store (neocortex): dense, overlapping, small weight delta per exposure, extracts structure across an ensemble. Rule: the fast store may NEVER write directly to the slow store, because a large single-item update overwrites accumulated structure (catastrophic interference). The only legal path is offline re-presentation of fast-store items INTERLEAVED with other items, many times, each time moving slow-store weights a little. Remote memory = accumulated residue of many small reinstatement-driven updates, which is why hippocampal damage spares it.
- **Sharp-wave ripple as the compressed, content-bearing replay packet**: The transfer unit. CA3 recurrent excitation fires a synchronous population burst; CA1 emits a fast oscillation of tens-to-~200 ms carrying the waking spike sequence re-emitted in TIME-COMPRESSED form. Compression is functional: a multi-second trajectory is squeezed into a single plasticity-eligible window so its whole sequence falls inside one learning event downstream. Two knobs: (a) duration is the graded control variable — longer ripples replay longer segments of a route and improve memory when prolonged; (b) CONTENT is not substitutable — artificially inducing ripples with the right envelope but no real sequence content produces no benefit. A replay batch is defined by its payload, not its shape. The selection bias decays across the rest period, so recency weights which items are eligible.
- **Nested slow-oscillation / spindle / ripple clock (hierarchical gating windows)**: A three-level phase hierarchy acts as a scheduler. Level 1: the neocortical slow oscillation (<1 Hz) alternates global down-states and up-states; only the up-state is a receptive window. Level 2: spindles are phase-locked to that up-state, subdividing it into slots. Level 3: hippocampal ripples are clustered into spindle troughs. Effect: hippocampal output is only delivered when cortex is depolarized and locally plastic, at sub-100 ms alignment precision. This is causal, not correlational — externally enforcing SPW-R/delta/spindle coordination after encoding manufactures consolidation on a task that would otherwise not consolidate. Coincidence of three independent oscillators is the commit condition.
- **Low-acetylcholine mode switch (direction of information flow)**: A global neuromodulatory mode bit. High cholinergic tone (wake) biases the circuit toward encoding: cortex → hippocampus, entorhinal input dominant, hippocampal recurrents suppressed. Minimum cholinergic tone (SWS) reverses the gain: hippocampal recurrent/output pathways dominate, permitting CA3-driven ripples to broadcast into neocortex. The same wiring runs in two directions depending on one scalar. Consolidation requires that the system be OFFLINE — disconnected from new input — for the reverse mode to be safe.
- **Bidirectional cortico-hippocampal addressing (cortex queries, hippocampus answers)**: Replay is not a blind flush of the buffer. Cortical activity in the ~100s of ms BEFORE a ripple predicts what the ripple will contain, and the ripple's content predicts the cortical activity that follows: a cortical→hippocampal→cortical loop. Read as a protocol: cortex emits a partial cue/query, hippocampus performs pattern completion and returns the matching episode, cortex integrates and issues the next query. This makes replay selection top-down steerable — the slow store decides what it needs from the fast store, rather than the fast store deciding what to push.
- **Targeted memory reactivation (external cue-keyed replay API)**: The query channel is addressable from outside. A sensory cue present at encoding, re-presented during SWS, selectively boosts consolidation of the memory it was bound to. Strict preconditions: the cue must have been present at encoding (an arbitrary cue does nothing); it must be delivered in SWS (ineffective in REM or wake); and it is selective for the store — hippocampus-dependent declarative memory is boosted, hippocampus-independent procedural memory is not. Computationally: an encoding-context tag doubles as a retrieval key for the offline consolidation pass, letting an external scheduler prioritize which items get replayed.
- **Global proportional downscaling with selective sparing (synaptic homeostasis)**: A renormalization operator, not a per-item delete. Wake produces net potentiation, which costs energy and supplies, lowers SNR, and saturates capacity. Sleep applies a MULTIPLICATIVE shrink: synapse size drops ~18% in proportion to current size, so ordering is preserved while absolute magnitudes and total cost fall. Crucially the shrink is SELECTIVE — the largest synapses and those with recycling endosomes are spared, revealing two pools: a large (~80%) weak/plastic pool that decays and a small (~20%) strong/stable pool that persists. No synapse is individually judged for deletion; survival is decided by relative position under a uniform contraction plus a protection predicate.
- **Replay embedded in downscaling → active forgetting as the generalization engine**: The two mechanisms compose into a compression operator. Replay selectively re-potentiates what fires; global proportional decay erodes everything else. The memory is therefore carried by the RATIO of replayed to unreplayed weights, not by absolute values — which means forgetting is not loss, it is the normalization step that makes the signal legible. Because replay repeatedly samples many episodes interleaved into the slow learner, what survives is the shared statistical structure across episodes and what dies is episode-specific detail. Measured output: after sleep, subjects recognize NOVEL items obeying the learned probabilistic structure, with gain proportional to slow-wave sleep obtained; consolidation yields abstracted, gist-like representations. Generalization is the product of forgetting, not a separate process.

- McClelland JL, McNaughton BL, O'Reilly RC (1995). Why there are complementary learning systems in the hippocampus and neocortex: insights from the successes and failures of connectionist models of learning and memory. *Psychological Review*. PMID:7624455. [verified] — supports: The two-store fast-learner/slow-learner architecture: memories are first stored in the hippocampal system; these changes support reinstatement in neocortex; neocortical synapses change a little on each reinstatement; remote memory is the accumulation of those changes. Connectionist models require gradual, interleaved learning to discover ensemble structure, so the hippocampus permits rapid learning of new items without disrupting that structure, and reinstatement interleaves new memories with old.
- Durrant SJ, Taylor C, Cairney S, Lewis PA (2011). Sleep-dependent consolidation of statistical learning. *Neuropsychologia*. PMID:21335017. ## ETTZ relevance notes from researcher
Bears directly on the owner's flagged sharpest gap and on several other design questions.

1) SLEEP REPLAY = memory-nightly, but ETTZ's fold is currently the wrong SHAPE. Biology does not fold once per night into durable state; it emits thousands of discrete, small, content-bearing replay packets (SPW-Rs, 50–200 ms, one trajectory each) delivered into gated windows over the whole night. The cold-fold-60s scaling pain on 71k envelopes is exactly the pathology CLS predicts for a monolithic re-derivation: the biological answer is incremental, per-item, interleaved re-presentation, not a full state render. A ripple-shaped memory-nightly would replay a bounded batch of envelopes per pass with an explicit selection policy, and the fold would become O(selected) rather than O(all).

2) THE SHARPEST GAP IS CONFIRMED AND HAS A MECHANISM. Biology's promotion of repeated episodic patterns into semantic/procedural form is not a separate 'generalize' job — it is the emergent product of (a) repeated interleaved replay into a slow learner plus (b) global proportional decay. Durrant 2011 and Klinzing 2019 show the output is gist-like and novel-instance-generalizing, and that the amount of slow-wave sleep predicts it. ETTZ has the replay substrate (events/ pulled from every outbox) and the slow store (claims/, procedures/) but no interleaved re-presentation loop and no decay against which survival is measured. Concretely: a nightly pass that samples recurring envelope motifs across flow refs and proposes CANDIDATE claims (as low-trial predicates awaiting +confirm/-fail) would be the missing hippocampus→neocortex path. The claims/ trials ledger is already the right target format — a predicate with a trial count IS a slowly-accumulated weight.

3) 'COMPRESS AND ARCHIVE, NEVER ERASE' IS COMPATIBLE WITH DOWNSCALING — and downscaling is the better model than microglial pruning. de Vivo 2017 and Tononi/Cirelli 2014 describe a MULTIPLICATIVE renormalization with selective sparing: no synapse is individually judged, ordering is preserved, and the largest/endosome-bearing ~20% are exempt. That maps cleanly onto ETTZ's law: implement decay as a monotonically declining SALIENCE SCORE on bank balances and episodic items, not as a delete. The item stays; only its rank falls. Quarantine's 30-day dwell clock plus grace verification is already a protection predicate — de Vivo's spared population is its biological twin. This gives a principled answer to 'is use-dependent pruning adoptable': yes, as rank decay under a uniform contraction, with quarantine as the sparing rule and CUSTODIAN's snapshot interlock as the final protection predicate.

4) FLOW REFS ARE THE REPLAY PAYLOAD, NOT THE TAG. A ripple replays an ordered sequence, compressed, as one unit — which is exactly a flow ref's 'journey reads as one thread'. Fernández-Ruiz 2019's finding that longer ripples replay more of a route and that randomly induced ripples with no real content give NO benefit is a direct warning: a fold that replays synthetic summaries rather than the actual ordered envelope sequence buys nothing. Replay whole flows, and let flow length set batch size.

5) TARGETED MEMORY REACTIVATION ANSWERS 'WHAT SELECTS WHAT GETS FOLDED'. TMR shows the consolidation pass is externally addressable by an encoding-context tag, and Rothschild 2017 shows cortex QUERIES the fast store rather than the fast store pushing. ETTZ has two ready-made query sources the fold does not currently use: the IGNORANCE GRAPH (open questions) and DESIDERATA (known gaps). Making memory-nightly cue-driven — replay preferentially the envelopes whose subject matches an open question or a desideratum — is the TMR analog and converts the fold from a flush into a directed query loop. The mandatory 'why' field is the natural cue key.

6) THE NESTED CLOCK MAPS TO BEATS + TRIGGER FABRIC. SO up-state → spindle → ripple is a three-level gating hierarchy where transfer commits only on coincidence of three independent oscillators. ETTZ has the ingredients (15s BEATS as the tonic carrier, n8n deciding WHEN, verb doors deciding WHAT, a confirming envelope closing the loop) but no phase relationship between them. A consolidation window that only opens when the mesh is quiescent — the low-acetylcholine analog, i.e. the fold runs only when organs are idle so hippocampal→cortical flow is not competing with new ingest — is a cheap, high-value borrowing.

7) TWO-STORE SEPARATION IS ALREADY RIGHT; THE INTERLEAVING DISCIPLINE IS MISSING. events/ (fast, append-only, one-shot, high specificity) and claims/procedures (slow, structured, checkable) is textbook CLS. McClelland 1995's warning is that the slow store must be updated a LITTLE per reinstatement and INTERLEAVED with other content — so a proposed claim should require accumulating trials across independent replays and across sources (BINNA's parent = union of children is the natural interleaving set), never a single-pass promotion from one high-frequency motif.

8) PROCEDURES' 'NO CLOCK' RULE IS BIOLOGICALLY WELL-FOUNDED but incomplete: biology also has no decay clock on consolidated procedural traces, yet it still REBUILDS them by replay. A procedure that is never replayed and never tried is not aging in ETTZ — biology would keep re-potentiating it during sleep only if it fires. The honest borrowing is: procedures don't decay, but replay should still re-verify them, so 'fails when tried' fires sooner rather than at the next real invocation.
