# myelination: Myelination and activity-dependent (adaptive) myelination: oligodendrocytes as a route-compilation layer — activity-gated wrapping of proven axonal pathways, conduction latency/synchrony tuning, myelin as consolidation of frequently used routes, and the cost/commitment trade-off that trades plasticity for speed

## Summary
Myelin is the CNS's route-compilation layer. One oligodendrocyte (OL) extends up to ~40-60 processes, each wrapping a single axon segment (internode, ~20-200 um) in tens of compacted membrane lamellae, leaving ~1 um nodes of Ranvier where Nav channels cluster. Current jumps node to node; conduction velocity rises roughly an order of magnitude and scales linearly with axon diameter rather than with its square root. Critically, the tunable parameters are not binary "myelinated / not": internode length, sheath thickness (g-ratio), node diameter, and the length of unmyelinated gaps each independently set arrival time.

THE WRITE SIGNAL. Electrically active axons release glutamate from vesicles along the axon shaft onto OPC AMPA/NMDA receptors and onto the ensheathing OL process. Mensch et al. showed in zebrafish that blocking vesicle release with tetanus toxin light chain reduces the number of sheaths an individual OL makes, while pentylenetetrazole-driven activity increases it — activity sets a per-cell sheath budget during a short (hours) formation window, after which the count is fixed. Wake, Lee & Fields identified the local tag: axonal glutamate nucleates cholesterol-rich signaling domains at the axon-glia contact and, via Fyn kinase, triggers local translation of myelin basic protein mRNA already pre-positioned in the OL process. A diffuse permissive signal (activity) is thereby converted into a site-specific commitment by a mark laid down at the contact. Two signals, two scopes, two timescales.

NECESSITY AND LATENCY-TO-COMMIT. Gibson et al. optogenetically drove premotor cortex in awake mice: OPC proliferation within hours, more OLs and thicker myelin over weeks in deep layers and corpus callosum, and improved limb function — all abolished when Myrf (myelin regulatory factor) was deleted in OPCs, which blocks new OL production without disturbing pre-existing myelin. McKenzie et al. made the same dissociation behaviorally: Myrf-blocked mice cannot master a complex running wheel, though old myelin is intact. Xiao et al. pinned the clock: Enpp6+ newly differentiating OLs appear 2.5 h after first exposure in subcortical white matter and 4 h in motor cortex, and blocking them impairs learning within that same 2-3 h window. New myelin is on the critical path in hours, not days.

NOT "FASTER" — "CORRECTLY TIMED". Ford et al. found auditory brainstem axons where larger-diameter (low-frequency) fibers carry *shorter* internodes and node diameter grows along distal segments — geometry tuned so spikes arrive at the calyx of Held at the right time, not the earliest time. Pajevic, Basser & Fields quantified the stakes: a 5 ms delay flips two coupled ~100 Hz oscillators from constructive to destructive interference, and sub-millisecond changes shift phase by ~30 degrees. Myelin is a programmable delay line, and delay is the computed quantity. Etxeberria et al. closed the loop in vivo: monocular deprivation — phenocopied by VGLUT2 knockdown in retinal ganglion cells — shortens internodes and measurably slows optic nerve conduction.

EDITING IS TWO-PHASE AND ADDITIVE. Bacmeister et al. imaged learning-activated motor cortex axons: during learning existing sheaths retract and nodes lengthen (modelled to slow conduction first), then after learning new sheaths are added, making intermittent myelination more continuous and faster. Hughes et al. found that in adult cortex new OLs myelinate previously bare or sparsely myelinated axons and, once integrated, sheaths are extremely stable; sensory enrichment adds OLs but does not lengthen existing sheaths. Growth is by appending new segments, not rewriting old ones.

CONSOLIDATION. Steadman et al. showed learning-induced oligodendrogenesis is required for memory consolidation and, mechanistically, for the learning-induced increase in hippocampal sharp-wave-ripple / cortical spindle coupling. Offline replay depends on conduction infrastructure laid down during the waking experience.

THE BILL. Harris & Attwell's energy budget: myelin's saving on action-potential ATP could repay construction within months, but maintaining the OL resting potential usually outweighs that saving — myelination buys speed and axonal metabolic support, not thrift. It also costs plasticity: myelin-derived Nogo-66, MAG and OMgp signaling through NgR1 consolidate circuitry and close the ocular-dominance critical period (NgR1-null mice remain juvenile-plastic at P120). Yeung et al.'s bomb-pulse 14C dating gives the human commitment horizon: callosal OL number is set in childhood with only ~1/300 annual cell exchange, while myelin membrane itself turns over rapidly. Topology freezes; content refreshes.

## Key mechanisms (computational logic)
- **Activity-gated route detection: axonal vesicular glutamate as the usage signal**: SIGNAL: glutamate released from vesicles along the axon shaft (not only at synapses) onto OPC AMPA/NMDA receptors and onto the ensheathing oligodendrocyte process; ATP/adenosine act as parallel carriers. THRESHOLD: graded, not all-or-none — tetanus-toxin block of vesicle fusion reduces sheath count per oligodendrocyte, PTZ-driven hyperactivity raises it. TIMESCALE: OPC mitogenic response within hours of stimulation; new differentiating (Enpp6+) oligodendrocytes at 2.5-4 h. COMPUTATION: this is a usage counter on a *route*, not a weight on a synapse. Only axons that actually carried traffic are eligible for wrapping; the pathway, not the endpoint, is the unit of credit.
- **Local translation tag: Fyn/MBP marks WHICH segment gets wrapped**: TAG: axonal glutamate nucleates cholesterol-rich (lipid-raft) signaling domains at the axon-glia contact and activates Fyn kinase, which de-represses local translation of MBP mRNA already trafficked into the oligodendrocyte process. LOGIC: a two-factor commit — a *global permissive* signal (the cell is in a myelinating state, activity is present) times a *local site mark* (this contact, this process). Neither alone writes myelin. This is the closest myelin analog to synaptic tagging-and-capture: the resource (MBP protein, membrane) is made cell-wide but captured only where the tag sits. TIMESCALE: minutes-to-hours for local translation; days for compaction.
- **Per-cell sheath budget with a bounded commit window**: Each oligodendrocyte has a short formation window (~5 h in zebrafish; a few days in mammals) during which activity sets how many sheaths it will ever make; after the window the count is fixed and the cell becomes near-permanent infrastructure. CONSEQUENCE: capacity allocation is front-loaded and irreversible per cell. To myelinate more routes later you must recruit a NEW oligodendrocyte from the OPC pool, not renegotiate an existing one's budget. Hughes et al.: newly integrated sheaths are extremely stable; enrichment adds cells rather than extending existing sheaths.
- **Latency engineering by geometry: internode length and node diameter as tunable delay parameters**: Conduction velocity is set by four independently adjustable structural knobs — internode length, sheath thickness (g-ratio), node diameter, and gap length — not by a single 'myelinated' bit. Auditory brainstem axons deliberately violate the naive scaling rule: low-frequency (large-diameter) fibers carry SHORTER internodes, and node diameter increases along distal segments, so that spikes arrive at the calyx of Held at a target time. OPTIMIZATION TARGET: correct arrival time, not minimum latency. Slowing a path is as legitimate an edit as speeding it.
- **Conduction delay as a first-class network computation (phase/synchrony)**: Delay is the computed variable, and the tolerance is brutally tight: a 5 ms delay flips two coupled ~100 Hz oscillators from constructive to destructive interference; sub-millisecond changes shift relative phase by ~30 degrees and materially change signal amplitude. Small myelin changes therefore have large, nonlinear effects on whether two regions can bind. The system is tuning arrival-time distributions across a bundle so that distributed populations phase-lock — a global timing-coherence objective implemented entirely through local per-segment edits.
- **Two-phase remodeling: destabilize (retract, lengthen nodes) then commit (add sheaths)**: On learning-activated axons, phase 1 = existing sheaths RETRACT and nodes of Ranvier lengthen, which modeling predicts initially SLOWS conduction; phase 2 = after learning, new sheaths are added, increasing continuous stretches of myelination and speeding conduction. Both magnitude and timing of nodal/myelin dynamics correlate with behavioral improvement. LOGIC: an explicit unlearn-then-relearn edit cycle, and critically the 'unlearn' step is retraction of a wrap — the axon is never destroyed. Destructive-looking plasticity is implemented as withdrawal of an overlay, not deletion of the substrate.
- **Consolidation gating: new myelin is a precondition for offline replay coordination**: Blocking learning-induced oligodendrogenesis (Myrf cKO in adult OPCs), while leaving all pre-existing oligodendrocytes and myelin intact, impairs consolidation of water-maze and contextual-fear memories AND abolishes the learning-induced increase in hippocampal sharp-wave-ripple / cortical spindle coupling. LOGIC: the nightly consolidation pass is not substrate-independent — its ability to coordinate two structures depends on conduction infrastructure built during the preceding waking episode. Route-building is a *daytime prerequisite* whose payoff is collected offline.
- **Commitment cost: myelin buys speed by spending plasticity and energy**: COST 1 (plasticity): myelin-derived Nogo-66, MAG and OMgp signal through NgR1 to consolidate established circuitry and terminate the critical period; NgR1-null mice retain juvenile ocular-dominance plasticity at P45 and P120, and the same signaling caps axonal regeneration after injury. Wrapping a route actively suppresses rewiring around it. COST 2 (energy): the ATP saved on action potentials could repay myelin construction within months, but the standing cost of maintaining the oligodendrocyte resting potential typically exceeds that saving — myelination need not save energy at all. COST 3 (horizon): human callosal oligodendrocyte number is set in childhood with ~1/300 annual exchange, while myelin membrane turns over fast. Topology is a decades-long commitment; content is refreshed continuously.

## ETTZ relevance notes from researcher
DIRECT HITS ON THE OWNER'S OPEN QUESTIONS.

(1) "Myelination-as-precomputation / Hebbian strengthening beyond usage balances" — this is the strongest mapping and myelin is the right analog, but with a correction: myelination is NOT synapse strengthening. It is ROUTE-level latency optimization of a path already proven to carry traffic. The unit of credit is the pathway, which in ETTZ is the FLOW REF, not the individual envelope. Design implication: derive a per-flow-ref recurrence count from BANK usage balances, and when a flow shape repeats above threshold, compile it — materialize the join, warm the index, precompute the fold slice for that route. The biological threshold is sustained repetition over HOURS (Xiao: 2.5 h to first commit), not a single event; and Gibson/McKenzie show the payoff is only collected if you build a NEW structure — reusing existing structures is provably insufficient.

(2) "Synaptic tagging = flow refs or claims' trials?" — Wake/Fields gives a cleaner answer than the synaptic literature does. Myelin's commit is TWO-FACTOR: a global permissive signal (the organ is in a writing state, activity is present) AND a local site tag (Fyn-gated local MBP translation at that one axon-glia contact). Map: claims' TRIALS (+confirm/-fail) are the global permissive credit; FLOW REFS are the local site tag that decides WHICH segment gets the optimization. Neither alone should authorize a precompute. That is a concrete two-key gate ETTZ can implement.

(3) "Sleep replay = memory-nightly" — Steadman is the load-bearing citation. Blocking daytime route-building did not merely slow consolidation; it abolished the learning-induced hippocampal ripple / cortical spindle COUPLING. Read against ETTZ: memory-nightly's fold quality is bounded by whether the day built the routes. The 60s+ cold fold over 71k envelopes IS an unmyelinated path — and the fix biology chose is not a faster fold, it is a compiled route laid down during the episode itself. Consider emitting fold-relevant precomputed slices at ingest time (from the envelope's own why/refs) rather than only at 03:00.

(4) "Use-dependent pruning vs 'compress and archive, never erase'" — myelination is the mechanism that lets ETTZ get pruning's BENEFIT without pruning. Microglia destroy; oligodendrocytes merely privilege. Making the proven path faster makes the unproven path relatively slower without deleting a byte. Even Bacmeister's apparent "weakening" phase is sheath RETRACTION with the axon left fully intact — an overlay withdrawn, not a substrate destroyed. That is precisely ETTZ's quarantine semantics, and it is biologically load-bearing, not a compromise. Recommend the owner treat myelination-as-precomputation as the primary adoptable answer to the pruning question.

(5) "Filopodia analog / what actively EXPLORES" — partial answer here: OPC processes survey many axons and most nascent sheaths are retracted rather than stabilized; the per-cell sheath budget (Mensch) is spent on trial ensheathments, most of which fail. The exploration mechanism is CHEAP SPECULATIVE TRIALS WITH HIGH RETRACTION RATE, funded from a bounded budget. ETTZ's DESIDERATA and IGNORANCE GRAPH name gaps but there is no equivalent budgeted speculative-probe loop.

(6) "Neurogenesis = new organs joining" — the OPC pool maps onto the registry-drop path, and ORGANON conformance levels (participant → organ → system) map cleanly onto OPC → premyelinating → myelinating oligodendrocyte. One caution from Mensch/Hughes: the commit window is BOUNDED and front-loaded — a cell's sheath count is set within hours of integration and never renegotiated. If ETTZ's watch-contracts propose a join, the new host's route-capacity allocation should be treated as an early, hard-to-revise decision, not something to defer.

(7) "Soma = organ core?" — supported. One oligodendrocyte soma supports up to ~40 independent sheaths on different axons: one core, many routes, each locally tagged. This is the fractal law (parent = union of children) at the cell scale.

(8) CAUTION ON THE COST/COMMITMENT SIDE — the sharpest warning for ETTZ. Harris & Attwell: myelin often does NOT save energy; the standing maintenance cost of the wrapping cell frequently exceeds the savings. McGee: myelin actively SUPPRESSES rewiring via NgR1 and closes critical periods. Yeung: the wrapping cells persist for decades with ~0.3%/yr turnover. Translated: every precomputed route, GRAPHIFY cache, and dated PHAROS snapshot carries a standing maintenance cost and creates resistance to re-derivation. Do not myelinate a route until its claim has accumulated enough +confirm trials, and keep the retraction path (Bacmeister's phase 1) as a first-class operation — a sheath you cannot retract is a critical period you cannot reopen.

(9) EXPLICIT NON-MAPPING — myelination is NOT the answer to the suspected sharpest gap (no mechanism promotes repeated episodic patterns into claims/procedures). Myelination is the OPPOSITE of generalisation: it entrenches and specialises one specific proven route, and Hu et al. document maladaptive myelination where aberrant activity locks in the wrong path (e.g. epilepsy). Adopting myelination-as-precomputation without a separate episodic→semantic promotion mechanism would make ETTZ faster at exactly the routes it already over-uses, and would deepen the generalisation gap rather than close it. The two mechanisms must be built as a pair.
